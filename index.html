What is a repair café?

A repair café is a free, recurring meet-up where people bring in broken belongings — lamps, chairs, jeans, blenders, toys — and skilled neighbors help fix them, side by side. It's run entirely by volunteers, free to attend, and powered by people who'd rather fix something than throw it away.

We're starting one for the Inner Richmond: a volunteer-run, neighbor-to-neighbor project to keep our belongings working, share knowledge and skills, and spend a weekend day fixing something together instead of replacing it.

Foggy Fixers is a volunteer-run repair café inspired by Repair Café SF and Mission Repair Café. We aim to bring the repair café movement to another corner of the city.

What can I bring?

Anything broken in these categories that you can carry is welcome, and has a good chance of getting repaired:

Electrical & electronic appliances Clean clothing Furniture Housewares Toys

Please read the house rules and waiver before you come — they cover a few important things about how the day works.

What we can't repair

Some things are out of scope, either because they're better handled elsewhere or because they're unsafe or impractical for us to take on:

  • Items still under warranty
  • Bicycles — many SF bike shops can help, and the SF Public Library runs a rotating bike repair clinic at branch libraries
  • Microwaves
  • Mobile phones — though we're happy to give advice on use
  • Earphones / earbuds
  • Cameras — though we might have some advice
  • Watches
  • Sewing alterations to clothes or curtains
  • Zips — usually too time-consuming
  • Leather stitching — needs an industrial sewing machine
  • Petrol-driven products
  • 3-phase power tools
  • Anything that could be hazardous to our repairers, such as items known to contain asbestos
  • Many toasters, which often can't be repaired

How it works

  1. 1

    Bring your broken item

    Participants bring in items that are broken or not working, fill out a short form, and sign a waiver. Check the list above for what we can and can't repair.

  2. 2

    Get matched with a repairer

    Skilled volunteers work one-on-one with you to:

    • Diagnose the problem
    • Attempt a repair using the materials and tools on hand at the café
    • If a repair isn't possible, point you toward where to go next or how to recycle the item
  3. 3

    Learn and connect

    Repair cafés are about learning and community as much as repair. The emphasis is on:

    • Fixing things together — this is collaborative, not a drop-off service
    • Sharing skills — you watch, help, and learn as your item gets repaired
    • Keeping good things in use and out of the landfill
